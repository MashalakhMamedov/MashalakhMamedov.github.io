About

I am studying Mechatronics Engineering at Tor Vergata, after a BSc in Electrical and Electronics Engineering. I work in MATLAB, Simulink and Simscape Electrical, and my strongest ground is power electronics: modelling a converter, validating it across its operating range, and understanding what the results actually mean.

What I have learned about myself is how I work when something breaks. On the inverter project the design passed at the easy resistive load and failed at the inductive one. I traced it to the output filter resonating against the load, fixed it with damping, and measured what the fix cost elsewhere. That sequence, finding the cause rather than the symptom and then being straight about the trade, is the part I would want to be judged on.

The projects here are written the same way: what I did, what a teammate did, what the numbers show and where they stop. I would rather hand you something you can check than something you have to take on trust.

Before university I spent nearly three years on a gas-pipeline construction site interpreting between vendor engineers and the crews, and later annotated autonomous-driving imagery, which is where the computer-vision interest started. I am looking for a six to twelve month internship from October 2026 where I can work on real systems alongside engineers with more experience than I have, and be useful while I learn.
